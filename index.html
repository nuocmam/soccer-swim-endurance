This is a long essay, not a session plan. It was written so that a parent, a coach, and a skeptical physiologist can share the same page: every kilometre, every beat per minute, every week-count that appears below was taken from pages that were actually fetched for two research briefs (soccer match physiology; water training). If a number is not in those files, it is not here. If a drill was not sourced — Wilber’s alternating hard/moderate deep-water runs, Bushman’s exclusive four-week block, Eyestone’s underdosed 20–30 minutes, Fonseca’s 944 aquatic jumps, Stolt’s NSCA cap of ≤60 minutes, Brody’s 25–40 minute lower-extremity circuit — it is not invented to fill a hole. If you are the parent of a club player in the 8–10 range

Youth club soccer · roughly 8–10 years

The World Cup and Premier League figures describe the sport, not a training target for a player in the 8–10 range. The American Academy of Pediatrics places the 6–10 band in the FUNdamental stage: agility, balance, coordination, speed; “positive and fun.” The next LTAD stage, Training to Train (10–14), is not a licence to import adult 90-minute aerobic volume.

The closest fetched youth match data are Sanchez et al. 2021: Spanish professional-academy Under-12 males, age 10.9 ± 0.8 years — adjacent to the 8–10 range, not interchangeable with it. Their official matches ran two 30-minute halves. Soccer-11 total distance was 4598 ± 1163 m, not 10–13 km. Treat those numbers as adjacent, not interchangeable.

AAP hour rules, stated as needing further long-term validation: organized hours per week greater than the athlete’s age in years, and an organized-to-free-play ratio greater than 2:1, have been associated with overuse injury. Recommendations on the fetched page: at least 3 months off the primary sport per year (in 1-month increments); 1–2 days off per week from the primary sport; multiple sports at least until puberty. Adding a dissimilar endurance mode on top of a 3 × 90-minute + weekend-match week is an extra-volume question those rules are designed to flag — not a physiology green light.

If you are thinking about adult / elite soccer

The demand profile of the sport

Mean internal load around 70% of VO2max and 85% of HRmax over a match, 150–250 brief intense actions, phosphocreatine cycling in the more-than-70% of time that is low-intensity, repeated-sprint ability, and a decade in which high-intensity and sprint distance have grown far faster than total distance: that is what the aerobic engine has to support. It is not a 10 km time trial.

Water can keep that engine turning when impact, heat, or a rehab constraint takes running off the table. Deep-water running at high, frequent intensity maintained land VO2max and 5 km performance in trained runners across 4–6 weeks (Bushman; Wilber). Swimming raised swimming VO2max and did not raise treadmill VO2max (Magel 1975). Tanaka 1994: minimum transfer from swim training to VO2max, and cross-training never exceeds the sport-specific mode.

Use water as extra aerobic work, rehab volume, or congestion-volume without impact. Do not use it as a substitute for pitch running, change of direction, or soccer high-intensity distance. No fetched front-crawl soccer RCT existed to copy.

Direct quotations appear only where the source briefs marked them as quotes. Helgerud et al. 2001 is now full-text verified (7-page MSSE PDF): run-based 4×4 moved match distance and sprints. Magel 1975 and Tanaka 1994 were fetched as abstracts and remain the swim-to-run counters.

First principles The argument

Five claims the rest of the essay has to earn

  1. Soccer endurance is intermittent running. Elite match play sits near 70% VO2max and 85% HRmax, with 150–250 intense actions, PCr resynthesis in the >70% of time that is low-intensity, and a modern demand curve in which high-intensity distance and sprinting have grown faster than total distance.
  2. Water is a different mechanical planet. Density is about 800 times that of air. Buoyancy offloads by depth. Hydrostatic pressure raises stroke volume. Deep-water running has no vertical ground-reaction force. Overload is drag, not grass. The heart-rate offset is a range, not a −10 bpm law.
  3. Therefore swimming and DWR can keep the aerobic engine (Wilber / Bushman: high-intensity, frequent DWR, 4–6 weeks) but do not train running stiffness, change of direction, or soccer high-intensity running. Magel: swim VO2max up, treadmill VO2max not. Tanaka: minimum transfer swim → run.
  4. Use water as extra aerobic work, rehab, or congestion-volume without impact — not as a substitute for pitch running.
  5. How much: sourced doses only. Front-crawl soccer RCT: none fetched.

I · The sport Soccer endurance is not a 10 km time trial

A 10 km time trial is a nearly continuous aerobic problem. A soccer match is a 90-minute problem in which most of the metres are cheap and a minority of the metres decide the game. The aerobic system is not optional — it is the background and the recovery machinery — but the quality that has grown in the tracking era is repeated high-speed running, not total distance.

What a World Cup team actually covers

Across all 64 matches of the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022, outfield players including substitutes (no goalkeepers, no extra time), teams covered 108.1 ± 3.6 km in total per match. High-intensity distance at ≥20.0 km·h−1 was 9.0 ± 0.9 km in the Biology of Sport paper, reported as 9,001 m on the FIFA Training Centre page and as 9,001 ± 850 m in the PMC results. Sprint distance at ≥25.0 km·h−1 was 2.3 ± 0.3 km in the abstract, 2,345 m on FIFA TC, and 2,345 ± 314 m in the PMC results.1, 2

“on average, teams during the FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022™ covered 108.1km in total, with 9,001m and 2,345m covered at the higher intensities (>20km/h and >25km/h), respectively.” FIFA Training Centre, Qatar 2022 physical analysis, Part 1 — distances teams covered2

Against Russia 2018 the same comparison is the essay’s first warning against “more easy kilometres.” Total distance was only +3%. Distance at the higher intensities was +16–19% in absolute terms. On a per-minute basis the high-intensity difference was still 9–12%, and total distance was 3% lower in Qatar 2022 than in 2018. The game did not get longer in metres. It got sharper.1

That sharpness is not evenly distributed. Top versus bottom teams on high-intensity distance (≥20 km·h−1): United States, Canada, Saudi Arabia, Germany, and IR Iran covered 19–34% more HID than Argentina, Ecuador, Qatar, Poland, and Costa Rica. Match-to-match coefficient of variation at team level: total distance 3.2%, HID 9.1%, sprint 13.9%. Japan’s HID/sprint CV ran 23.2–29.4%. Ecuador’s HID CV was 2.1%; Uruguay’s sprint CV 3.3%. High-intensity runs correlated with defensive transitions and recoveries (r = 0.63) and with progressions and final-third entries (r = 0.73–0.75).1 HID is not decoration. It is how teams win the moments that look like tactics on television.

Position is a different sport wearing the same shirt

Bradley’s Part 1 paper on Qatar 2022 used 722 full-match observations across eight roles. Defensive and central midfielders covered 8–19% more total distance than other roles. Some midfielders (Brozović, Skhiri) exceeded 13 km. A centre-back example: Mepham >11 km; Silva 8 km. Wide midfielders and wide forwards produced HID 16–92% higher and sprint distance 36–138% higher than centre-backs, defensive midfielders, central midfielders, and centre-forwards. Average HID for those wide roles sat around 1.0–1.1 km; upper examples Castagne and Taremi around 1.6–1.8 km. Central roles: 0.5–0.8 km HID.3

Possession context splits the same high-intensity metres. Defensive and central midfielders did 71–83% of their HID out of possession. Attacking midfielders, wide forwards, and centre-forwards did 55–68% of theirs in possession. Nine of the top ten sprint speeds came from wide roles, in the band 35.3–35.7 km·h−1. Mean top speeds: wide defenders / attacking midfielders / wide midfielders / wide forwards / centre-forwards 32.1–32.3 km·h−1 against centre-backs / defensive midfielders / central midfielders at 30.2–30.8 km·h−1.3

Second-half running, per minute, among full-match players: total distance down about 7% versus the first half, more pronounced in defensive midfielders (−9.8%), least in wide defenders (−6.0%). HID/sprint decline 6–10% on FIFA Training Centre, 8–10% in Bradley’s discussion. Attacking midfielders’ HID/sprint fell −17.5% to −20.0%. At team level, including substitutes, there was no half-by-half difference at the higher intensities; total distance per minute was still higher in the first half.3, 4, 1 Substitutes are part of how modern teams keep HID alive. They are not a swimming problem.

The speed zones themselves, TRACAB Gen5 at 25 Hz: Zone 1, 0.0–6.9 km·h−1; Zone 2, ≥7.0–14.9; Zone 3, ≥15.0–19.9; Zone 4, ≥20–24.9; Zone 5, ≥25.0. HID is Zones 4+5; sprint is Zone 5.3 Those thresholds are adult elite. They are not the zones used on a U12 GPS unit, and they are not an 8–10-year-old’s homework.

A decade of the Premier League: total distance barely moved; sprinting did

Allen, Taberner, Zhilkin and Green tracked English Premier League running loads across 2015/16–2024/25: 3,750 games, 3,408 after exclusions, team totals including goalkeepers. Their high-intensity definition is >5.5 m/s; high-speed running 5.5–7 m/s; sprint >7 m/s. Over ten seasons, team totals: total distance +2%, HID +27%, HSR +23%, sprint distance +40%. Intensity per minute (HID/min, HSR/min, sprint/min) increased; TD/min decreased (effect size −0.27).5

The largest jump sat in 2023/24, linked to the added-time rule (+5.0 minutes mean game duration versus 2015/16). A 2024/25 added-time modification reduced minutes (−1.8 min versus the prior season in the paper’s Table 2 discussion). Rolling three-season total match minutes were +1,600; congested minutes +1,415. Top-six clubs: total minutes +5,659 (+9%), congested +2,003. Congested minutes as a share of total: all players 13% → 26% versus the first three-season average; top-six stayed 17–29% against all-player 12–20%. The paper cites other work that some elite players have as few as 22 rest days between seasons and average 4.3 days between games.5 That is a player-health problem. It is not a call to add a fifth aerobic mode.

EURO 2024, classic match analysis, and the women’s game

UEFA’s 18 March 2025 article on EURO 2024 recorded Kylian Mbappé’s top speed at 36.7 km/h; Nico Williams, Micky van de Ven, and Benjamin Šeško at 35.9 km/h. Substitutes performed more high-speed running per minute than starters. Peak effort was more frequent at the start of both halves, then increasingly concentrated toward the end of matches.6

“Although time spent in these higher speed zones accounts for a small proportion of the overall distance covered by a player in a game, sprinting can be vital to important match actions and is the most physically demanding action performed by players.” UEFA.com, Physical demands of EURO 2024, 18 March 20256

The classic picture, from Bangsbo’s GSSI Sports Science Exchange #125 (which itself cites Mohr 2003, Bangsbo 1991/1994/2006, Krustrup 2005/2006), has not been repealed by GPS. Typical top-level outfield male distance: 10–13 km. The majority is walking and low-intensity running. Players perform low-intensity activities for more than 70% of the game. International top-class versus lower professional (Mohr et al. 2003 as stated in SSE #125): 28% more high-intensity running (2.43 vs 1.90 km) and 58% more sprinting (650 vs 410 m). Elite players perform 150–250 brief intense actions per game. English top three standards (Bradley et al. 2013a as stated there): high-speed running >19 km/h — Championship 803 m, League 1 881 m, Premier League 681 m; sprinting 308 / 360 / 248 m. FA Premier League: about 700 turns per game, around 600 of them 0–90° (Bloomfield et al. 2007). About 110 actions with the ball. Tackles 3–27, jumps 1–36 (Mohr et al. 2003). Elite female high-intensity running about 30% lower than elite male (Krustrup 2005; Mohr 2003, as stated in SSE #125).7

That Premier League less high-speed running than Championship and League 1 is worth sitting with. Possession tactics, not inferior fitness, is the attribution on the page. More aerobic volume does not automatically produce better soccer. Work rate is not monotonically “more metres = better.” Bradley notes that some elite forwards in his sample (Messi, Mbappé, Lewandowski) sat in a low-volume, low-explosiveness quadrant.

FIFA Women’s World Cup 2023 methodology (absolute team kilometres were not on the fetched page): female-specific zones, high-intensity >19.0 km/h, sprint >23.0 km/h. High-intensity running increased by around 20% between Canada 2015 and France 2019, as cited on that page. Absolute 2023 distances were not compared across tournaments because of a different tracking system.8

Yang and Feng (2026) unified 225 matches and 3,454 outfield players across the 2010–2022 World Cups: LIRD <15 km/h, HIRD ≥15 km/h, sprint >25 km/h. All confederations showed increases in top speed, sprint, and LIRD. Total distance rose 2018→2022 in AFC, CONCACAF, and UEFA; CONMEBOL total distance changed little. HIRD was lower in 2018/2022 than in 2010/2014 in AFC, CONMEBOL, and UEFA. The paper cites Tuo et al. that in 2018 approximately two-thirds of match distance was walking or very low speed, and only about 7–8% of match distance was >15 km/h. Those 7–8% / two-thirds figures are Tuo as reported by Yang; the original Tuo paper was not fetched.9

Soccer numbers in turf green The demand that swimming would have to support is not “cover 10–13 km.” It is: tolerate ~70% VO2max for 90 minutes while producing 150–250 intense actions, ~700 turns, role-specific HID of roughly 0.5–1.8 km at ≥20 km·h−1, and top speeds in the 32–36+ km·h−1 band — then do it again when the calendar says 4.3 days later. Water can tax the engine. It cannot be those actions.

II · Energetics Ninety minutes of phosphocreatine cycling

Once the GPS file is put down, the internal load is almost embarrassingly aerobic — and the decisive spikes are not. That is not a contradiction. It is the sport.

“The aerobic energy system is highly taxed during a football game, with average and peak heart rates around 85% and 98% of maximal values, respectively, corresponding to average oxygen uptake of around 70% of maximum.” Bangsbo, GSSI Sports Science Exchange #125, key points7

Average heart rate during elite match play ~85% HRmax; peak ~98% HRmax; corresponding average VO2 ~70% VO2max (Reilly & Thomas 1979; Ekblom 1986; Ali & Farally 1991; Bangsbo 1994; Krustrup et al. 2005 — all as stated in SSE #125). Core temperature 39–40°C during a game supports that aerobic loading estimate (Ekblom 1986; Mohr et al. 2004). Heart rate is rarely below 65% HRmax, so muscle blood flow stays elevated versus rest.7

ATP–PCr, glycolysis, glycogen

The 150–250 brief intense actions imply high anaerobic turnover in periods of the game. Creatine phosphate is largely resynthesized in subsequent low-intensity periods (Bangsbo 1994, as stated in SSE #125). Muscle CP after intense in-game periods is about 75% of rest; biopsies were taken 15–30 s after activity, so in-match CP is lower. Bangsbo estimates that with clustered intense bouts, CP may fall below 30% of rest.7

Average blood lactate 2–10 mM, individual values >12 mM (Krustrup et al. 2006, as stated in SSE #125). Muscle lactate after intense periods about 15 mmol/kg dry weight (fourfold rest), highest 35 mmol/kg d.w. — less than one-third of values in short-term exhaustive intermittent exercise. Blood lactate often reflects the accumulated response to several high-intensity actions, not a single bout.7

Muscle glycogen is the main substrate. Single-fibre analysis after a game: a significant number of fibres depleted or partly depleted (Krustrup et al. 2006). Saltin (1973) as cited: if pre-match glycogen was low (~45 mmol/kg w.w.), stores were almost depleted by halftime; with normal pre-match ~100 mmol/kg w.w., some players were <10 mmol/kg w.w. at full time. Other studies: 40–65 mmol/kg w.w. remaining after the game. FFA in blood rises, more in the second half; fat oxidation increases as glycogen falls.7

Temporary fatigue is not a VO2max ceiling in isolation. After the most intense 5-minute period, high-intensity running in the next 5 minutes was 12% below game average (Mohr et al. 2003, as stated in SSE #125). End-game: high-intensity running in the last 15 minutes was 35–45% lower than the first 15 minutes. Substitutes covered 25% more high-intensity ground in the final 15 minutes than other players.7 Adding low-skill endurance in a second sport can deplete glycogen and add tissue load without training the 150–250 intense running actions or the ~700 turns. That sentence is a synthesis of fetched facts, not a swimming trial.

Repeated-sprint ability is not a VO2max test

Gist, Northey, Cooper, Gloeckner and Peacock (2017) tested Division I soccer players, n=20 (10 male / 10 female). RSA protocol: 10 × 30 m, start every 30 s; work:rest about 1:4 (~6 s work, ~24 s rest). Male VO2max 66.3 ± 5.1 ml·kg−1·min−1; female 51.7 ± 5.9. Average sprint time male 4.7 ± 0.2 s, female 5.4 ± 0.3 s. Fatigue index male 6.5 ± 1.4%, female 5.3 ± 1.9%. VO2max versus every sprint time: r ≤ −0.69, all P < 0.001; versus average sprint time r = −0.767. No significant relationship between VO2max and fatigue index (r = 0.333, P = 0.15). Percent of HRmax during the RSA test: 73% (sprint 1) → 87% (sprint 5) → 93.5% (sprint 10).10

The authors’ interpretation on the fetched page: greater aerobic capacity is associated with faster repeated 30 m sprints; they cite the aerobic system’s role in PCr replenishment. They also note prior studies (Aziz; Pyne) that found no VO2max–RSA link at 20–30 m with 1:4 to 1:5 rest — so the literature is mixed, and this paper’s contribution is a significant association even at distances under 40 m. Gist also notes that discrepancies between studies include mode (running versus cycling), sprint distance, age, and level: transfer is mode-sensitive even within RSA tests.10

Match work:rest is not a single ratio. Low-intensity activity occupies more than 70% of time, but mean VO2 is still about 70% VO2max because of the 150–250 intense actions. That is intermittent play with clustered high-intensity periods and then a temporary drop-off. The laboratory ~1:4 RSA protocol is a test, not a match average.7, 10

III · Aerobic capacity What VO2max actually buys

Slimani et al. 2019, a network meta-analysis of 16 studies and 2,385 male players, ages 10–39 as stated on the page, is the fetched synthesis of soccer VO2max. Overall male soccer VO2max 48–62 ml·kg−1·min−1 regardless of competitive level (authors’ synthesis). Stratified ranges on the page: elite 59.2–63.2; international 59.2–61.5; professional 58.2–62.2; amateur 57.8–61.7 ml·kg−1·min−1. Higher-level versus lower-level: effect size 0.58 (95% CI 0.08–1.08), P = 0.022. The authors call VO2max “the most powerful discriminator between higher and lower-level soccer players” in this analysis. Heterogeneity is high (I2 93–98%). Tests are mixed lab and field. Male only.11

By position: goalkeepers 48.4–57.5; defenders 53.2–62.8; midfielders 54.7–63; forwards 54.5–62.9 ml·kg−1·min−1. Midfielders versus goalkeepers ES 1.37; defenders versus goalkeepers ES 1.31; forwards versus goalkeepers ES 1.07. Midfielders versus defenders or forwards were not significant.11 The engine is larger in the roles that cover the most grass. That does not make the engine the game.

Age pattern on the same page: VO2max increased year-to-year between Under-10 and Under-15 in pairwise/meta-regression (significant increases U10 vs U11, U11 vs U12, through U14 vs U15, and U16–18 vs U20–23). U14 > U15 (ES −0.98) and U14 > U16 (ES −1.18) in the pairwise set they report — the U14 node was higher than U15/U16 in those comparisons. No significant U15 vs U16 or U16 vs U17. Season: VO2max higher off-season and lower at end of season when stratified by level. Competitive-season values higher than pre-season and end-season when stratified by age.11 This meta-analysis does not isolate recreational or club players in the 8–10 range; samples include amateur through elite, ages 10–39.

College Division I snapshot from Gist 2017: males 66.3 ± 5.1, females 51.7 ± 5.9 ml·kg−1·min−1. The authors note that 60 ml·kg−1·min−1 has been used as a men’s professional minimum, citing Stolen 2005 on that page.10 No fetched page gave female soccer VO2max bands by level; they are not invented here. Female match high-intensity running about 30% lower than elite male is verified via SSE #125.

SSE #125’s more interesting claim is kinetic, not ceiling: more important than average match VO2 may be the rate of rise of VO2 in short intense actions; oxygen kinetics are limited by local muscle oxidative capacity; interval training can speed the VO2 rise (Krustrup et al. 2004, as stated there).7 Slimani: “well-developed aerobic fitness helps soccer players to maintain repetitive high-intensity actions within a soccer match, to accelerate the recovery process…” Gist: VO2max correlated with the speed of each sprint in an RSA test, not with fatigue index; mechanism discussed: the aerobic system assists PCr–ATP recovery between sprints. Bradley Part 1: “This type of training [position-specific high-intensity drills] has been found to tax both the aerobic and anaerobic systems and thus create desirable adaptation to develop players physiological capacities” (citing Ade 2014; Fransson 2018; Mohr 2016).3, 11, 10

Running economy and match-output transfer from run-based HIIT are now full-text verified in Helgerud et al. 2001 (see the run-HIIT section). That protocol is running, not swimming.

IV · SAID Specificity on grass

No fetched page was a swimming randomized trial in soccer players. The specificity argument has to be assembled from pages that were fetched, and then from Magel and Tanaka in the water brief.

Bradley Part 1 conclusion: “practitioners should ideally design high-intensity drills that replicate both position- and individual-specific characteristics that fully prepare players for the match demands.” FIFA Training Centre background: physical analysis is meant to “aid the development of specific training drills that replicate aspects of the game.” High-intensity activity is involved in goal-scoring opportunities (the page cites Faude et al. 2012; Faude’s own 360-goal / 83% / 45% figures were not on a fetched full text and stay Unverified).3, 12

Sanchez et al. 2021 is a specificity finding wearing a youth-GPS coat. Smaller area per player in Soccer-7 and Soccer-8 versus Soccer-11 changed the external-load mix: more relative accelerations and decelerations in 7/8; more total and high-speed distance in 11. Format-specific load is itself SAID.13

Implication for swimming as soccer endurance support, as logic from fetched facts, not as a swim-soccer trial: soccer’s metabolic load is intermittent running with accelerations, decelerations, turns (~700), and 150–250 intense actions on grass, at mean ~70% VO2max / ~85% HRmax, with PCr cycling during the >70% low-intensity time. A dissimilar mode does not reproduce (i) stretch-shortening and eccentric decelerations, (ii) running economy, (iii) the VO2-on kinetics of running transitions, (iv) the speed-zone distribution FIFA now treats as the evolving demand — HID and sprint up far more than total distance.

V · Physics Water is a different mechanical planet

The temptation is to treat the pool as a kinder treadmill: same run, less pounding. It is not. Density, buoyancy, hydrostatic pressure, and the absence of a vertical ground-reaction force in deep water change the problem being solved. A soccer player who “goes hard” in the pool is solving a drag-and-hydrostatics problem. A soccer player who goes hard on grass is solving a ground-reaction, stretch-shortening, and change-of-direction problem. The cardiovascular system can be invited to both rooms. The tendons and the ankles cannot.

Density and buoyancy by depth

Di Prampero (1986), as reported on the fetched Kravitz/Mayo aqua review, states that water density is approximately 800 times that of air, which raises the energy cost of moving limbs through water.14 That is the first mechanical fact. Air is not a serious fluid for a running stride. Water is.

Becker (2009), fetched in full PDF: mean human specific gravity 0.974; lean mass near 1.1, fat mass about 0.9. A body with specific gravity 0.97 floats when 97% of volume is submerged. Neck-depth immersion: only about 15 lb of compressive force (approximate weight of the head) on spine, hips, and knees. Immersion to the symphysis pubis offloads 40% of body weight. Immersion to the umbilicus offloads approximately 50%. Xiphoid immersion offloads 60% or more, depending on arm position.15

Harrison et al. (1992), as quoted on a fetched PMC page: gravitational force reduced by 30% at knee-level, 50% at waist-level, and about 90% reduced weight-bearing at the neck.16 Harrison and Bulstrode (1987), as reported in a fetched orthopaedic aquatic-loading study: C7/neck 5.9–10% of dry body weight; xiphisternum 25–37%; ASIS/navel 40–56%. The same paper’s own measurements in periarticular-fracture patients (n=61): mean percent dry weight 7.9% (SD 3.3) at neck exhaling, 6.0% (SD 2.7) at neck inhaling, 23.9% (SD 5.9) at nipple, 51.4% (SD 5.6) at navel.17

Kwok et al. (2022) define deep-water running as running with 70% of the body immersed, submerged at shoulder level, with or without a flotation device. Deep water eliminates vertical ground-reaction forces.18 Shallow-water work is not the same planet as deep water. At xiphoid or chest depth the legs still load at roughly one-quarter to one-third of dry weight when standing, and more when walking or jogging. Nagle et al. note that walking and jogging may increase weight-bearing by about 25% to 75% depending on depth, movement, and speed — citing Gulick/Geigle via Nagle 2017.19

Neck / C7 — remaining load about 6–10% of dry weight (Harrison & Bulstrode); Bressel: 7.9% exhaling, 6.0% inhaling; Becker: ~15 lb compressive. Harrison 1992: ~90% reduced weight-bearing. This is DWR territory; Kwok: 70% immersed, shoulder, zero vertical GRF.
Xiphoid / nipple — Becker: 60% or more offloaded. Harrison & Bulstrode: 25–37% remaining at xiphisternum. Bressel nipple: 23.9% of dry weight. Nagle restates xiphoid offload ~60%.
Umbilicus / navel / ASIS — Becker: ~50% offload at umbilicus. Harrison & Bulstrode: 40–56% remaining. Bressel navel: 51.4% of dry weight. Harrison 1992: 50% reduction at waist.
Symphysis pubis — Becker: 40% of body weight offloaded.
Knee — Harrison 1992: gravitational force reduced 30% at knee-level. This is not “non-impact.”

VI · Hydrostatics Heart rate in water is a range, not a law

Immersion is a cardiovascular intervention before it is an exercise session. Becker, fetched PDF: water pressure 22.4 mm Hg per foot of depth, or 1 mm Hg per 1.36 cm. Immersion to 48 inches is about 88.9 mm Hg — slightly above typical diastolic pressure. Neck-depth immersion: central blood volume increases by about 0.7 L (60% increase in central volume); cardiac volume increases 27–30%; mean stroke volume increases 35% at rest (resting SV ~71 mL/beat plus ~25 mL → ~100 mL). Heart rate typically drops as filling rises; at average pool temperatures the rate lowers by 12–15% from symphysis to xiphoid immersion. Cardiac output increases by about 1,500 mL/min at clavicle-depth immersion. Neck-depth: systemic vascular resistance falls 30% at thermoneutral temperatures; systolic blood pressure during work generally about 20% less in water than on land. Work of breathing for a 1 L tidal volume increases 60% at neck submersion (about 6–9% drop in vital capacity versus xiphoid).15 Nagle restates the same chain, including the 12–15% heart-rate decrease at xiphoid in about 33.5–35.5 °C water.19

The slogan that follows this physiology is “heart rate is about 10 bpm lower in water.” It appears on fetched pages. It is not a law.

VII · Force Drag, not ground reaction

Deep water: no foot contact, therefore no vertical GRF, no land-style push-off (Kwok; Becker). Propulsion is against viscous drag, which rises as a log function of velocity under turbulence (Becker). Horizontal and multi-plane drag is the overload, not ground reaction.15, 18

Shallow water and aquatic plyometrics still have a floor, but it is a quieter floor. Donoghue, Shimojo and Takagi (2011), full PMC: water depth 1.3 m (about 3 cm below xiphoid for their sample), 27.5 °C, drop jump from 30 cm. Peak impact forces 33–54% lower, impulse 19–54% lower, rate of force development 33–62% lower versus land for most drills. Peak GRF land 4.32–6.77 BW versus water 1.99–4.05 BW. They note land running typically 2–3 BW on one leg (citing Cavanagh).21 That is a bridge after running, before full land plyometrics — and the opposite of a stiffness stimulus. Reduced RFD is not how a soccer ankle learns to be a soccer ankle.

Becker is explicit: aquatic training is “unlikely” to substantially improve dry-land coordination skills (hurdles, high jump, complex timing).15 Killgore et al. (2006), cited by Becker: a cross-country DWR style is closer to treadmill knee/ankle kinematics than a high-kick style. Kwok (citing Silva 2020): DWR showed higher activation of distal leg muscles than proximal thigh muscles versus land treadmill running — so muscle emphasis is not a land-run copy.18 NSCA’s Stolt, in the 2008 aquatic cross-training roundtable: footwork (jumps, hops, shuffles, sprints, change of direction) can be introduced in water but “should not be solely relied on” because buoyancy and viscosity change the drill. Higher-impact shallow-water work: wear water shoes.22

Frangolias, Rhodes and Taunton (1996), on the Kravitz page: experienced DWR athletes (at least 6 DWR workouts per month for 6 months) had a land–water VO2max difference of 3.8 mL/kg/min; inexperienced 10.3 mL/kg/min. Experienced runners: no significant HRmax land versus water difference. Frangolias and Rhodes (1995), same page: max VO2 and HR about 8% lower in water in experienced DWR runners. Butts, Tucker and Greening (1991): DWR VO2max 9% lower in men, 16% lower in women versus treadmill; HRmax 5% lower both sexes. Brown et al. (1997): at matched cadences, submax VO2 was higher in DWR than treadmill (harder at the same turnover); max VO2 on the treadmill was 13% (men) and 24% (women) higher than DWR.14 Reilly, Dowzer and Cable (2003) abstract, quoted on Becker and Kwok: at submax, lactate higher in DWR than treadmill at a given VO2; at max, VO2, VE, and HR decreased in water; still an “adequate stimulus” for cardiovascular training. Aerobic performance maintained with DWR up to 6 weeks in trained endurance athletes; sedentary individuals improve VO2max more than athletes.15, 18 Chu and Rhodes (2001) abstract: immersion to the neck lowers respiratory and cardiovascular parameters; VO2max and HR lower during DWR versus land; DWR training produced “equivocal” responses versus land in fit/highly trained, mixed for untrained.23

VIII · Transfer that is real Wilber, Bushman, and the underdose warning

The honest aerobic story in water is not “swimming makes you a soccer player.” It is: hard, frequent deep-water running can hold a runner’s land engine for a handful of weeks; easy, short, land-heart-rate-zone water work can fail; untrained people can gain VO2max in the pool at three sessions a week. Those sentences are all sourced. They are runner sentences. They are still the best fetched evidence for using water as an aerobic stand-in.

Soccer in-season cannot copy the 5–6 d/wk exclusive DWR runner studies without dropping soccer-specific load — and no fetched paper tested that trade-off.

IX · The counters Magel 1975 and Tanaka 1994

Deep-water running is still a running action, even when the ground has been taken away. Front crawl is not. The two classic transfer papers sit in the water brief as fetched abstracts. They are the essay’s load-bearing counters. They are not Unverified.

Magel et al., 1975. Fifteen male recreational swimmers; interval swim 1 h/day, 3 days/wk, 10 weeks. Swim VO2max +380 mL/min; max VE +14.9 L/min; max swim time +4.0 min; max HR −3.5 bpm. No significant improvement in treadmill-running VO2max. “The results … clearly demonstrate the specificity of the cardiorespiratory adaptation to swim training.”27

Tanaka, 1994. Review of transfer among cycling, running, and swimming. Some VO2max transfer exists, “more noticeable when running is performed as a cross-training mode.” Quote, as marked in the water brief: “Swim training, however, may result in minimum transfer of training effects on VO2max.” Cross-training effects never exceed those induced by the sport-specific training mode. Specificity has “greater significance, especially for highly trained athletes.” Rehab and overreaching are the justified use-cases.28

What does transfer from swimming, on fetched pages: central cardiovascular adaptations can occur with swim training (stroke volume, resting HR, VO2 in the swim mode). Martin et al. (1987), as reported by Nagle: 12 weeks, 3×/week, swim continuous + interval plus land circuit; peak supine VO2 +10%; peak supine LV end-diastolic volume +18%; lower resting HR.19 Becker: neck-depth work raises inspiratory muscle work; competitive swimmers show larger VC/TLC. Useful for respiratory endurance, not soccer-specific locomotion. Hydrostatic edema control and non-impact aerobic volume: Becker; Kwok.

Bone is not a swimming gift to a soccer player. Becker reviews impact versus swim: youthful swimmers generally less BMD than gymnasts and impact athletes, higher than sedentary; Bravo aquatic “impact” in waist-depth water did not increase spine or femoral-neck BMD over a year. Soccer needs impact-sensitive bone and tendon. Ankle stiffness, stretch-shortening, change of direction: no fetched RCT showed swimming improved soccer COD or ankle stiffness. Donoghue: aquatic landings cut RFD 33–62% — the opposite of a stiffness stimulus. Skill limiter, Brody/Thein excerpt: many athletes “are not accomplished swimmers and may not be able to achieve an adequate cardiovascular workout” with front crawl; they recommend kickboard flutter/dolphin as a workaround.26

Soccer-specific swimming RCTs for endurance: none fetched that used front-crawl volume as the independent variable in soccer players. Football aquatic literature that was fetched is mostly plyometrics, aqua aerobics, and hydrotherapy, not endurance swimming (Doctor and Rathod 2024).

X · Football in the pool Soccer’s aquatic literature is plyometrics, not endurance swimming

Doctor and Rathod (2024) screened 1,512 records and kept 10 football studies (2012–2022), ages 12–30. They conclude aquatic therapy improved speed, agility, and leg power. They explicitly found no studies on core strength or kick velocity. Endurance was not the primary outcome set.29 Johnson et al. (2021), aquatic versus land plyometrics: 8 studies in athletes; APT typically 6–12 weeks, 2–3 days/week; similar jump gains; APT may cut impact and soreness. The soccer sample is Fonseca.30

It is not an aerobic-engine study.

Water polo–style treading as a soccer aerobic method: not found. In-water soccer or water-polo drills for field-sport VO2: not found. Flutter kick only as aerobic cross-training: thin, practical only — Brody excerpt: flutter or dolphin with flippers or kickboard can condition unskilled swimmers; no soccer RCT. Paddles, drag suits, extra surface area: mechanics yes (Becker: at a given speed, larger surface area → more overload; Nagle: gloves/paddles as HIIT overload), soccer RCTs no. Deep-water treading: not found as a dosed protocol. Bungee-tethered aqua sprints: mentioned on non-fetched blogs; Unverified.

Bottom line, as the water brief stated it: aquatic plyometrics have a small soccer evidence base for power and agility. Aquatic endurance for soccer is inferred from runner DWR studies, not from water-polo or in-water soccer trials. Evidence for polo-style treading, in-water soccer, and drag-suit programming as soccer endurance tools is thin.

Sanchez U12 data (10.9 ± 0.8 years) sit above the 8–10 band and already show that even official matches are about 3–4.6 km, not 10–13 km. Adding a dissimilar endurance mode on top of 3×90-min + weekend match is an extra-volume question the AAP hour-versus-age and 1–2 days off guidance is designed to flag — not a physiology green light. There is no evidence-based “missing aerobic engine” in an 8–10-year-old club player that swimming must fill. The documented youth risks are overuse, early specialization, and adult-format load.

XIII · When the grass is the problem Rehab, heat, congestion

The strongest story water tells is not athletic glory. It is: do not rest for three to six weeks if you can keep the engine turning without grinding the tissue that is injured.

  1. Rest of 3–6 weeks can cost about 14–16% VO2max (Coyle via Brody excerpt), with significant cardiovascular decline from as little as 3 weeks inactivity.
  2. Exclusive DWR 4 weeks (Bushman) or 6 weeks at high matched intensity (Wilber) maintained land 5 km / VO2max / economy in runners.
  3. Underdosed DWR (20–30 min, 70–80% land HRmax, Eyestone; steady 80% HRR, Quinn) allowed VO2max to fall.
  4. Kwok: DWR viable for athletes susceptible to repetitive stress injuries; buoyancy eliminates vertical GRF.
  5. Thein and Brody (1998) JOSPT commentary (abstract fetched; book excerpt fetched): pool during rehab and post-recovery as adjunct; Michaud-style 8 wk, 3×/wk, 63–82% land HRmax, 16–36 min sufficient to increase VO2max in that study.
  6. Bressel 2015: anatomic-landmark depths let clinicians stay inside partial-weight-bearing limits (neck ~6–8%, nipple ~24%, navel ~51% of dry weight).
  7. Donoghue: aquatic plyos as a bridge after running (~2–3 BW) before full land plyos (4–7 BW).
  8. Soccer APT (Fonseca): power gains with less DOMS — useful in high-volume or return-to-jump phases, not a substitute for land COD.

No fetched soccer return-to-play guideline gave a week-by-week aqua aerobic ladder. Doses above are runner and mixed-athlete data applied cautiously.

Heat and extra volume without extra impact, as physiology, not as a FIFA heat protocol: water conducts heat 25 times faster than air (Becker). Cool pools (27–29 °C public; Reilly/Kwok 26–28 °C for max DWR) dump heat. Extra aerobic minutes in deep water add metabolic work with zero vertical GRF (Kwok). Becker: aquatic treadmill VO2 3 times greater at 53 m/min than land walking at that speed — slower locomotion can still be hard. This is walking/jogging in water, not a soccer drill. No fetched FIFA/UEFA heat document prescribed swim minutes as the extra-volume method. CWI after matches is a different intervention.

Congestion: the UEFA-reported Andersson study found active recovery (unspecified as aqua on that page) no better than rest across 72 h between two matches. Do not claim aqua jogging is a proven MD+1 performance restorer from that page. Allen et al. frame rising Premier League minutes and congestion as a player-health problem (fewer rest days, more high-speed load), not a call to add more endurance work. Extra aerobic sessions compete with recovery that the same literature says is already short.

XIV · Harm Risks that are real

Shoulder overuse — if using stroke swimming, not DWR

StatPearls Swimmer’s Shoulder (fetched 2023 update): 90% of swimming propulsive force from shoulder torque; elite weekly volume 60,000–80,000 m ≈ 30,000 strokes/arm; incidence 3–70% depending on definition; about 35% when defined as pain that interferes with training in elite/senior swimmers; some studies about 91% career prevalence. Soccer players doing small front-crawl volumes are not elite swimmers, but poor technique plus sudden yardage is a named trigger.36

Missed soccer-specific stimulus

Magel 1975; Tanaka 1994; Becker on coordination; Donoghue on reduced RFD; NSCA Stolt: water footwork should not be solely relied on. Every hour in the pool that displaces an hour of position-specific running is an hour the HID/sprint qualities — the ones that actually grew — are not trained.

Swimming skill as intensity limiter

Brody excerpt: unskilled swimmers cannot hit cardiovascular targets with crawl. Frangolias: inexperienced DWR under-performs max tests (VO2 gap 10.3 versus 3.8 mL·kg−1·min−1). NSCA: comfort before deep water. Stolt: confirm ability before head-deep work. Youth drowning incidence in soccer aqua sessions was not quantified on a fetched soccer-aqua paper; it is not invented as a rate. The safety sentence that is sourced is the NSCA one.

Asthma / chlorine

Fetched environmental-health abstract (Andersson et al. 2018): trichloramine 0.020–0.55 mg/m³ (mean 0.15); early-life pool exposure associated with higher pre-school asthma onset, especially in sensitized children.37 Helenius numbers (17% new asthma over mean 9 years in 29 Finnish elite swimmers; 28/29 non-asthmatic at career start) appeared on a fetched OAJSM-style page snippet — treat 17% / 9 years as Unverified unless the full Helenius paper is opened (it was not). Safe statement from fetched Andersson 2018: indoor chlorinated pools are an airway-irritant environment; swimming should not be assumed “always better for asthma” without considering trichloramine.

XV · Steelman Two counters against “just add water”

The essay’s own briefs asked for the case against extra aerobic work, and then for the case against extra volume. Both are sourced. A parent who only reads the pool physics will over-buy swimming. A parent who only reads the World Cup HID tables will under-buy the engine. Hold both.

Counter A — more aerobic work is not what soccer, especially modern soccer, needs

Intensity, not volume, is what has evolved. Qatar 2022 versus 2018: only +3% total distance but +16–19% high-intensity/sprint (per-minute still +9–12% HID/sprint, and TD/min actually −3%). EPL 2015/16–2024/25: TD +2%, sprint +40%, and TD/min fell while HID/HSR/sprint per minute rose (Allen et al., BJSM 2025). Yang and Feng 2026: sprint and top speed up across confederations while broader HIRD often did not rise. If the constraint is repeated high-speed actions, steady extra aerobic kilometres — especially non-running kilometres — miss the demand that actually grew.

High-speed running is a small fraction of distance but a large fraction of decisive actions. UEFA EURO 2024 said so in so many words. FIFA TC cites Faude et al. 2012 that goal-scoring opportunities typically include high-intensity activity. Bradley: wide roles do the HID/sprint; some elite forwards sat in the low-volume, low-explosiveness quadrant. GSSI/Bangsbo: Premier League players covered less high-speed running than Championship and League 1 (681 vs 803 vs 881 m >19 km/h), attributed to possession tactics, not inferior fitness. More aerobic volume does not automatically produce better soccer.

RSA literature is mixed on VO2max. Gist 2017 found a strong VO2max–sprint-time correlation, but the same page reviews Aziz (no association, 6×20 m, 1:5) and notes fatigue index did not correlate with VO2max even in their own data (r = 0.333, P = 0.15). Slimani’s claim that VO2max is “the most powerful discriminator” is from a heterogeneous male meta-analysis (I2 >93%) mixing field estimates and lab tests. Raising VO2max in a non-specific mode can look good on a treadmill without changing the quality that matters: first-step acceleration, 30–35+ km·h−1 sprints, and PCr recovery while running.

Magel and Tanaka then close the door on the most tempting substitution. Interval swim training 1 h × 3 d/wk × 10 wk raised swimming VO2max by 380 mL/min and swim time by 4.0 min, and did not significantly raise treadmill-running VO2max. Tanaka: minimum transfer from swim training to VO2max; cross-training never exceeds the sport-specific mode; specificity matters more in the highly trained. Town and Bradley: DWR max only 73.5% of treadmill VO2max. Donoghue: aquatic landings cut impact and RFD. Becker: water will not rebuild land coordination skills. NSCA: do not rely solely on water footwork.

Counter B — too much extra volume, especially year-round and extra-mode, is the risk

Elite adults are already volume-constrained by the calendar. EPL rolling 3-season minutes +1,600; congested minutes +1,415; top-six +5,659 minutes and a higher congestion share. Allen et al. frame this as a player-health problem, not a call to add more endurance work. Extra aerobic sessions compete with recovery that the same literature says is already short. Some elite players: as few as 22 rest days between seasons; 4.3 days between games.

Youth: hours, specialization, and format already load the tissues. AAP: overuse about 46–50% of youth athletic injuries in some reports; dropout 70% by 13; delay specialization until 15–16 for most sports; ≥3 months off primary sport; 1–2 days off/week; caution when weekly organized hours exceed age in years. Sanchez U12: Soccer-11 already produces greater total distance, high-speed distance, and absolute ACC than 7/8; relative eccentric/ACC load is higher in the smaller formats. A player in the 8–10 range (AAP FUNdamental, 6–10) who already trains in a 3×90 min + match pattern (the Sanchez academy week, not claimed as any one child’s week) does not have an evidence-based missing aerobic engine that swimming must fill.

Glycogen and temporary fatigue are not fixed by more easy aerobic metres in a different sport. SSE #125: end-match fatigue tracks fibre-specific glycogen depletion and clustered intense bouts (CP, ion handling), not a low VO2max ceiling in isolation. Adding low-skill endurance can deplete glycogen and add tissue load without training the 150–250 intense running actions or the ~700 turns.

XV · The contrast that settles the argument Run HIIT moved soccer. Swim training did not move running VO2max.

A later full-text pass fetched Helgerud et al. 2001 (7-page MSSE PDF), Helgerud et al. 2007 (7-page MSSE PDF), and Stølen et al. 2005 (37-page Sports Medicine PDF). Magel 1975 and Tanaka 1994 remain abstract-verified. Together they are the first-principles punch: the protocol that changed soccer match output was running at 90–95% of maximal heart rate. The protocol that changed swimming VO2max did not change treadmill VO2max.

Helgerud 2001 — elite junior soccer, full text

Nineteen male elite junior players (age 18.1 ± 0.8 yr) from two Norwegian teams; six were Norwegian national junior players. Random assignment within each team: training group n = 9, control n = 10. Regular week: four × 1.5 h practice plus one game. Added interval running: four times 4 min at 90–95% of fcmax, separated by 3 min jogging at 50–60% of fcmax, twice per week for 8 weeks. Controls did extra technical work in those slots. Treadmill tests at 3° inclination.38

Lactate threshold 47.8 → 55.4 mL·kg−1·min−1 (+16%). Running economy +6.7% (0.75 → 0.70 mL·kg−0.75·m−1). Whole-game %fcmax 82.7 → 85.6. No change in 10 m / 40 m sprint, 1RM bench, 1RM 90° squat, vertical jump, kicking velocity, or technical kicking points. The paper’s own conclusion: enhancing maximal oxygen uptake improved soccer performance as distance covered, work intensity, sprints, and involvements. The same paper says endurance training for soccer should ideally be carried out using the ball — and that 90–95% fcmax is hard to organise in a match, especially in lower divisions. This is a running result. It is not a swimming result. It is not an 8–10 protocol (the sample is 18.1 ± 0.8 yr).

Helgerud 2007 — students, not soccer, full text

Forty moderately trained male university students, 3 d·wk−1 for 8 weeks, four groups matched for total work. 4×4 min at 90–95% HRmax: VO2max 55.5 → 60.4 (+7.2%). 15/15: +5.5%. Long slow distance (45 min at 70% HRmax) and lactate-threshold continuous work: no significant VO2max change. Stroke volume rose about 10% in the interval groups only.39 Intensity, not just minutes, is the aerobic signal. That is why Eyestone’s easy water work failed and Wilber’s hard DWR held.

Stølen 2005 — physiology of soccer, full text

Stølen, Chamari, Castagna, and Wisløff, 37-page review: typical elite outfield distance about 10–12 km; average intensity 80–90% HRmax; male soccer VO2max spanning roughly 50–75 mL·kg−1·min−1 depending on level and position. The review discusses 4×4 programming, including an unpublished 10-day report of 62 → 66.5 mL·kg−1·min−1. Treat that 10-day figure as the review’s own citation of unpublished data, not as a new trial fetched here.40

Magel 1975 and Tanaka 1994 — the swim side of the same coin

Magel et al. 1975 (abstract fetched): 15 male recreational swimmers; interval swim 1 h/day, 3 days/wk, 10 weeks. Swim VO2max +380 mL/min; max swim time +4.0 min; max HR −3.5 bpm. No significant improvement in treadmill-running VO2max. Tanaka 1994 (abstract): swim training may result in minimum transfer of training effects on VO2max; cross-training effects never exceed sport-specific training; specificity has greater significance for highly trained athletes.

Put the two columns on one table: run-HIIT in soccer players moved match metres and sprints. Swim-HIIT in swimmers moved swim VO2max and did not move running VO2max. Deep-water running, when hard and frequent, can maintain a runner’s land VO2max for 4–6 weeks (Wilber, Bushman). That is the honest job of water for a soccer athlete: keep the engine when the pitch is the problem. It is not how you build the engine in the first place.

XVII · Close The engine and the pitch

What a 90-minute soccer match actually asks, verified, elite adult:

  1. Volume. About 10–13 km per outfield player; team outfield sum about 108 km at World Cup 2022. Most of it is <15–20 km·h−1.
  2. Intensity that distinguishes and decides. About 0.5–1.8 km per player ≥20 km·h−1 depending on role; team about 9 km ≥20 and about 2.3 km ≥25; 150–250 brief intense actions; top speeds 32–36+ km·h−1; about 700 turns.
  3. Energetics. The aerobic system supplies the background and the recovery (mean ~70% VO2max, HR ~85/98% max/peak, CP resynthesis in the >70% low-intensity time). Anaerobic systems supply the decisive spikes (CP possibly <30% rest in clusters; blood lactate often 2–10 mM). Late-match fade tracks glycogen in individual fibres plus a temporary post-bout drop (−12% HIR in the next 5 min).
  4. Fitness qualities. VO2max in male players typically about 48–63 ml·kg−1·min−1 by role/level in Slimani’s synthesis, higher in some D1 samples; it helps RSA sprint times in at least one fetched study but is not the same as running economy, VO2 kinetics, or eccentric/COD capacity.
  5. Specificity. Governing-body and match-analysis papers prescribe position-specific high-intensity running drills, not generic aerobic volume. HID/sprint have grown much faster than total distance.

What a club player in the 8–10 range actually plays, verified from adjacent published ages, not one named team: U12 official matches about 3.1–4.6 km depending on 7/8/11 format; Vmax about 23 km·h−1; high relative ACC/DEC in small formats. AAP: ages 6–10 = FUNdamental / multi-sport sampling; not Training to Win; watch weekly hours versus age, 1–2 days off, 3 months off primary sport.

Physics of the other planet: neck/deep water about 6–10% dry weight (Bressel/Harrison) or about 15 lb on the joints (Becker); xiphoid about 24–35% standing load; DWR zero vertical GRF. Hydrostatic loading raises stroke volume (about +35% at rest, neck) and usually lowers HR; offset is condition-dependent (10 bpm on Becker’s aquatic treadmill line; 10–15 bpm in 18–25 °C water on Nagle’s page; 12–15% HR drop with depth; Svedenhag HRmax 172 vs 188). Density about 800 times air. Drag, not grass.

Aerobic transfer that is real: exclusive, hard, runner-style DWR can hold land VO2max / 5 km / economy 4–6 weeks (Bushman, Wilber). Easy, short, land-HR-zone DWR can fail (Eyestone −4.9% VO2max). Untrained people gain VO2 at about 3×/week. Swimming is not running: Magel — +380 mL/min swim VO2max, no treadmill VO2max gain. Tanaka — minimum transfer from swim to run VO2max. Soccer literature: aquatic plyometrics have RCTs for jump/speed/agility; aquatic endurance swimming for soccer does not. Water polo / treading / drag suits: thin. Governing bodies: NSCA expert cap ≤60 min and “don’t rely only on water COD.” ACSM is general health minutes. FIFA/UEFA/UKSCA: no fetched dose. Front-crawl soccer RCT: none fetched.

The gap swimming would have to close, if it were the tool, is not “more minutes of cardio.” It is (a) oxidative recovery between running sprints (PCr resynthesis, VO2-on kinetics in the legs that sprint), (b) tolerance of 90 minutes of intermittent running including eccentric braking, (c) without stealing recovery or adding overuse on an 8–10-year-old already in a 3×/week + match week. Water can keep the engine. Water cannot make the pitch.

So: if a trained adolescent or adult soccer player cannot run — bone stress, congestion heat, a calendar that has already eaten the rest days — deep-water running at a Wilber/Bushman-like intensity, prescribed by RPE and cadence rather than a −10 bpm slogan, is a sourced way to keep VO2max from falling 14–16% in six weeks of rest. If the same player can run, the fetched match-analysis papers want position-specific high-intensity running drills. If the player is in the 8–10 range, the fetched AAP page wants fun, multiple sports, 1–2 days off, three months off the primary sport, and a wary eye on weekly hours versus age. The pool is a bridge. It is not a soccer player.
